Not attempted on purpose: **Hacker News (Show HN)**. It is one shot, the thread lives or dies
on the maker answering comments in the first two hours, and an unattended post spends the
opportunity. The angle that would work there is the privacy one — a face-cropping tool where
the face never leaves the tab — not the country coverage.

## The open-source directories, now reachable

The project is **AGPL-3.0** as of 2026-08-07 and the licence is live on GitHub, which is what
these directories check. opensourcealternative.to is done — filed as the open alternative to
Passport Photo Online, which is the comparison worth owning. Still open:

- OpenAlternative — same idea, needs an account
- awesome-privacy and similar GitHub lists — pull request, and they check the licence

Describe it as "open source (AGPL-3.0)". Do not describe it as "free to use however you like":
the whole point of the AGPL here is that a competitor may compete with this code only in the
open, and a network service built on it has to publish its own source.

## The pitch

**Name:** VisaYes · **URL:** https://visayes.app · **Contact:** info@superduperai.co

**Headline (5–8 words)**

> Free visa photos, made inside your browser

**One line**

> Free visa and passport photos, cropped to each country's published specification inside your
> browser. Nothing is uploaded, no account, no watermark.

**Full description**

> VisaYes makes visa and passport photos that match the published specification, for free,
> without uploading anything. The crop, the background removal and the face alignment all run
> inside your browser tab. The photo never reaches a server, there is no account, no watermark
> and no paywall. The only thing that travels is the neural network, downloading to your device
> once and then running there.
>
> It covers 24 documents across 21 countries — US visa and DV lottery, Schengen, UK, Canada,
> China, India, Turkey's ikamet, the UAE, Japan, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam and more — in 9
> languages, including Hindi, Chinese, Arabic and Turkish. Every dimension comes from a single
> registry checked against the issuing authority's own published specification, so the number
> printed on the page and the number the tool crops to cannot drift apart. Where a country runs
> an official checker of its own, the date that checker accepted our output is stated on the
> page.
>
> There is also a checker of our own: drop in a photo you already have and it measures head
> height, eye line, background uniformity and file size against whichever document you pick,
> and tells you what it found.
>
> Built because the existing tools charge for a crop, watermark the preview, and ask you to
> upload your face to find out whether it fits. None of that is necessary. Open source,
> AGPL-3.0.
